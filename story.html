Behind the Story

It started with a name

The phrase “Ella Umber” has the kind of sound that invites a second look. Add “Umber-Ella” and the name becomes a small piece of wordplay before the story has even begun.

That was the starting point: create a children's book whose title already feels like a game. From there, the character and the world could grow around the language.

Why weather?

Weather is familiar to every child. It changes the way a day feels, changes what people do, and provides an endless supply of observations. Rain can be inconvenient, funny, dramatic, or beautiful depending on where you are standing.

For a story built around imagination, weather is useful because it is both ordinary and unpredictable. It gives the characters something real to react to while leaving plenty of room for exaggeration and humor.

Why a messy dog?

Because somebody has to make a mess.

The dog adds physical comedy to the book and gives Ella something immediate to respond to. Children understand the particular logic of a pet that can turn a normal situation into an event. The mess also keeps the story grounded. Even in a world of wordplay, somebody still has to clean up.

The tone

The goal was never to make the story complicated. It was to make it memorable. The book uses playful language, a warm central character, and ordinary details that can be viewed from an unusual angle.

That combination leaves room for a child to enjoy the story at face value while an adult reading along can notice the wordplay and construction underneath it.

A small world on purpose

Some children's books build enormous fictional universes. Ella Umber does not need one. The story works because its world is close enough to everyday life that the unusual parts stand out.

The hope is simple: that readers finish the story smiling, remember Ella's name, and perhaps start playing with words of their own.
