The Book

A little story with a very unusual name

Ella Umber & Her Umber-Ella is a playful children's story built around a simple idea: language can be funny all by itself. A name can sound like a riddle. A familiar word can become a new one. An ordinary day can become an adventure when a child looks at it from a slightly different angle.

Ella's world combines that wordplay with weather, imagination, family warmth, and one particularly messy dog. The result is intended to be lighthearted rather than complicated, with enough silliness for children and enough verbal mischief for adults reading along.

What the story is about

At its heart, the book is about curiosity. Ella notices things, reacts to them, and lives in a world where the boundaries between the ordinary and the ridiculous are pleasantly thin. The story does not require a grand lesson before a reader is allowed to have fun. It starts from the idea that a good children's book can simply make a child laugh, wonder, or ask what happens next.

The weather provides part of the setting, the dog provides much of the disorder, and Ella provides the point of view. Together they create a small world where names, objects, sounds, and situations can be turned around and looked at again.

Why wordplay?

Children naturally experiment with language. They make up words, alter names, repeat sounds, and discover that changing one small piece of a sentence can change the entire feeling of it. Ella Umber & Her Umber-Ella leans into that instinct.

The title itself is part of the joke and part of the invitation. It asks the reader to slow down, say it aloud, and wonder what an “Umber-Ella” might possibly be.

The larger idea

The book is deliberately small in scale. It is not trying to explain childhood, solve a social problem, or turn every page into a lesson. It is trying to create a memorable character and a warm, funny little place to visit.
