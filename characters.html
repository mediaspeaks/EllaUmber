Meet the Characters

Ella Umber

Ella is the center of the story and the reader's guide through its odd little corners. She is curious, observant, and willing to follow an idea simply because it sounds interesting. That quality gives the story its sense of movement. Ella does not need a giant adventure to become engaged with the world. Sometimes a strange name, a change in the weather, or a dog doing something ridiculous is enough.

She is also the sort of character who gives children room to recognize themselves. Her world is imaginative without being disconnected from everyday life, and her reactions help turn ordinary events into something worth noticing.

The Umber-Ella

The title's “Umber-Ella” is part of the book's playful language. It is deliberately mysterious, inviting the reader to say the word, hear the similarity to Ella's name, and wonder where the story is taking it.

Rather than treating the title as a formal explanation, the book lets the joke become part of the experience. The name is an invitation to play with language, which is exactly what the story does.

The Very Messy Dog

Every good children's story needs a source of disorder, and this dog takes the assignment seriously. The dog's messiness adds physical comedy to a story that otherwise lives heavily in language and imagination.

The dog is not simply there to be cute. The character creates the kind of ordinary chaos that children understand immediately: something gets messy, somebody has to deal with it, and the situation becomes funnier the longer it continues.
